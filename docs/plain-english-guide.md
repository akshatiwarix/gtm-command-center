# GTM Command Center — how it works, in plain English

No code in this one. If you've ever opened three different tools to answer one
question — "which account should I call today, and why?" — this is written for
you.

## The problem, in one paragraph

A sales or GTM team usually has this information scattered across three places:
a fit score somewhere (built once, rarely revisited), a feed of "things that
happened recently" somewhere else (funding rounds, hiring sprees, leadership
changes), and a pile of notes or research nobody reads until a deal is already
moving. Nobody has all three in one place, next to a ranked list they can
actually trust. This tool exists to put fit, timing, evidence, and a ranked
order on one screen — and to make the ranking itself something you can inspect,
not just believe.

## 1. A fit score alone doesn't tell you when to act

Imagine two companies. Both are a perfect match for what you sell — right
size, right industry, right region. One of them just raised a big funding round
and posted twenty new job listings this month. The other hasn't done anything
notable in six months. A fit score alone can't tell them apart — they'd both
score the same. But only one of them is actually worth calling today.

This tool keeps fit and recent activity as two separate, visible numbers — a
**Fit score** (is this the right kind of company) and a **Signal score** (did
something happen recently that makes this the right *time*) — so you can see
both at once instead of picking one lens and losing the other.

## 2. A ranked list you can't see inside is a ranked list you shouldn't trust

"This account is #4 on the list" is a sentence that means nothing on its own.
Is it #4 because it's a great fit? Because something urgent just happened?
Because someone's been emailing them for months? If you can't answer that, you
either trust the number blindly or ignore it — and both of those are bad
outcomes that come from the same root cause: the ranking is a black box.

Every account here comes with a visible breakdown of its three ingredients —
Fit, Signal, and Engagement — plus a plain sentence explaining which one is
actually driving its rank. Nothing about the order is hidden.

## 3. What "priority" means should be something you can change, not something baked in

A ranking built around "companies that are a good long-term fit" and a ranking
built around "companies where something is happening right now" are genuinely
different rankings — and which one you want can change week to week. A
promotional push might call for leaning hard into recent activity. A
relationship-building quarter might call for leaning into fit instead. Most
tools bake this choice in once and make it expensive to revisit.

This tool puts three sliders on the screen — one for Fit, one for Signal, one
for Engagement — and every account's rank recalculates the moment you move one.
Drag "Signal" all the way up and watch companies with recent funding or hiring
news jump to the top, even ones that weren't a perfect fit on paper. Drag it
back down and the list settles back toward long-term fit. Nothing is hidden
behind a settings page you have to find first.

## 4. The evidence behind a score should be checkable, not just asserted

A score by itself is an assertion. "This account has 82 engagement points" begs
the question: based on what? Every account page here comes with a short list of
plain-English research notes — the actual signal that happened ("raised a $42M
Series B, 42 days ago"), the actual fit facts ("201-1000 employees, Fintech,
tech stack match"), and the actual engagement numbers ("3 meetings booked, last
touch 7 days ago"). Every one of those notes points at a real, specific fact
about that account — nothing generated, nothing summarized by an AI model, just
the underlying data written out in a readable sentence.

## What this tool doesn't try to do

It doesn't watch the real world for signals — every signal here is part of a
fixed, synthetic dataset built for this demo, not a live feed of real company
news. It doesn't let you save a custom weight setup and come back to it later —
moving the sliders is a live "what if I cared about this instead" lens, not a
saved configuration. And it isn't a CRM — there's no way to add a note, log a
call, or change an account's data from here. It's a lens over a fixed set of
accounts, built to show what "fit + timing + evidence + a ranking you can
inspect, on one screen" actually looks like.
