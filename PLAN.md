---

## Problem

GTM teams don't lack data about their target accounts — they lack one place that
holds all of it in a shape a rep can act on. Three failures show up over and over:

**1. Fit and timing live in different tools, so nobody sees both at once.** A
static ICP/fit score (built once, rarely touched) tells you a company is a good
match; a signal feed (funding, hiring, leadership moves) tells you something
changed recently. A rep who only has the fit score works well-matched accounts
with zero urgency behind them. A rep who only has the signal feed chases anything
that moved, regardless of whether it's actually a fit. Neither view alone answers
the only question that matters: *which account should I work right now, and why?*

**2. Prioritization is usually a black box.** "This account is ranked #4" means
nothing if nobody can see what put it there. Reps either trust a score blindly or
ignore it — both are bad outcomes, and both come from the same root cause: no
visible breakdown of what a score is made of.

**3. When leadership wants to change what "priority" means, there's no lever.**
A promo push that should temporarily favor buying signals over long-term fit, or
a quarter that should favor high-engagement accounts close to a decision, requires
someone to manually re-score a spreadsheet. There's no live way to ask "what does
the list look like if we weight timing higher this week?"

This repo's subject is those three failures: one screen that holds fit, recent
signals, a visible score breakdown, evidence-backed research, and a reweightable
priority ranking — together, live, and explainable.

### What this repo is not

- **Not connected to any other day's code.** Thematically closest to Day 001 ICP
  Score (fit scoring), Day 005 Signal Scout (signal monitoring), Day 006 Account
  Brief (research synthesis), and Day 017 TAM Calculator / Day 016 Territory
  Builder (prioritization-adjacent) — fully standalone, no shared module, no
  cross-repo import. Linked from the README only.
- **Not a CRM.** No write path, no persistence beyond the committed corpus, no
  account creation/editing.
- **Not a signal-detection engine.** Signals are pre-generated facts attached to
  each account at corpus-build time, not derived by watching any external source.
  (Day 005 Signal Scout is the day for signal *detection* logic.)
- **Not an LLM research tool.** Research bullets are deterministic templates built
  from an account's own seeded signals and firmographics — no model call, no free
  text generation. Same spirit as the rest of the series (LLM-based work is
  reserved for Phase 3, Day 041+).
- **Not real/live data.** A committed synthetic corpus, generated by a documented,
  seeded model. The claims this repo makes — that fit + signals + engagement
  combine into an explainable, reweightable priority ranking — are about the
  *system*, not about any real company.
- **Not a "bring your own weights and save it" tool.** Weight sliders recompute
  the ranking live in the browser; there is no save/share/persist step. That's a
  named post-MVP idea, not part of this build.

## Intended user

A GTM/RevOps lead or AE deciding which accounts to work this week, who wants one
screen that shows *fit*, *what changed recently*, *the evidence*, and *the ranked
order* — without switching between an ICP spreadsheet, a signal alert inbox, and a
research doc, and without taking a priority order on faith.

Secondary user: an interviewer or engineer reading the repo, who should be able to
see the corpus's generative model, every scoring formula, and the research/
explanation logic without running anything.

---

## User journey

1. Land on the **Command Center**. 200 synthetic target accounts, their signals,
   scores, and research are already precomputed. No upload, no sign-up, no config
   to set.
2. **Account table**: every account with its Fit / Signal / Engagement sub-scores,
   composite **Priority Score**, top recent signal, and a one-line research
   snippet. Sorted by Priority Score descending by default; the top 10 rows carry
   a visible "Top Priority" badge. Sortable by any score column; filterable by
   industry, size, region, and signal presence.
3. **Weight sliders**: three sliders (Fit / Signal / Engagement) above the table.
   Dragging one live-recomputes every account's Priority Score and re-sorts the
   table, client-side, from the same pure function the server used to compute the
   default-weight view.
4. **Global signal feed**: a compact, chronological, cross-account panel — the most
   recent signals across all 200 accounts, each linking into its account.
5. Click an account to open its **detail page**: full signal timeline, an SVG
   score-breakdown chart (Fit / Signal / Engagement / Priority), the account's
   research bullets (evidence-style, derived from its own signals and
   firmographics), and a plain-English "why this rank" explanation.

No accounts/save/share step — nothing here is user-authored input, only a live
lens (the sliders) over a fixed corpus.

## MVP scope

**In:**

- Seeded synthetic corpus: 200 target accounts, fixed seed, each with firmographic
  fields, 0–8 signals, and synthetic engagement stats, committed to the repo as
  JSON.
- Three deterministic sub-scores per account — **Fit**, **Signal**, **Engagement**
  — and a composite **Priority Score** (weighted sum, default weights 0.4 / 0.35 /
  0.25), all 0–100, all pure functions (see *Method*).
- Live client-side reweighting: three sliders recompute Priority Score and re-sort
  the table without a server round-trip, reusing the exact scoring function used
  server-side.
- Deterministic research bullets and a "why this rank" explanation per account,
  both derived only from that account's own committed data.
- Two screens: **Command Center home** (table + sliders + global signal feed) and
  **account detail** (timeline, score breakdown chart, research, explanation).
- `GET /api/v1/accounts`, `GET /api/schema`.
- `npm run sweep`: nine invariants, no network.

**Out (explicitly):**

- Cross-repo integration with any other day's code or data.
- Any live API call, model call, or real CRM data.
- Signal *detection* — signals are committed facts, not derived from watching a
  live source.
- LLM-generated research or explanations — deterministic templates only.
- Save/share/persist for slider weights or filters (client-side session state
  only, resets on reload).
- A separate "priority queue" screen — the default-sorted, badged table is the
  queue.
- Per-account API route — the single `/api/v1/accounts` payload already contains
  every account's full computation; the detail page is a client-side lookup into
  the same result.
- CSV export, saved views, search-as-you-type (see *Post-MVP*).

## Data sources

**A single committed synthetic corpus**, generated by `data/generate.ts` from a
fixed seed, checked into the repo as JSON. No real data was considered: this
repo's claims are about how fit, timing, and engagement combine into an
explainable ranking — properties of the scoring system, not of which company name
is on the record. The generator is committed and the seed is fixed, so the corpus
is fully auditable.

## System / architecture

Six layers, dependency arrows pointing only downward.

```
data/               corpus generation (accounts + signals, seeded RNG) + committed JSON + zod load schema
  ↓
lib/domain/         Account, Signal, ScoreBreakdown, Weights, ResearchBullet, CommandCenterResult — types + zod
  ↓
lib/scoring/        fitScore, signalScore, engagementScore, priorityScore(breakdown, weights) — pure functions
  ↓
lib/research/       research bullet derivation + rank explanation — pure functions over (Account, Signal[], ScoreBreakdown)
  ↓
lib/command-center/ orchestration — scores every account, builds the global signal feed, assembles CommandCenterResult
  ↓
app/                two screens (home + account detail) + /api/v1/accounts + /api/schema
```

### Rules of the architecture

1. **`lib/scoring/` and `lib/research/` are pure and deterministic.** Same corpus
   + same weights ⇒ byte-identical output. No `Date.now()`, no unseeded
   `Math.random()`. Signal recency is computed from a committed `asOfDate` in the
   corpus metadata, not from the real current date, so output never drifts.
2. **Nothing below `app/` knows about React, HTTP, or the DOM.** `priorityScore`
   must run identically in the browser (for the live weight sliders) and in the
   API route.
3. **`priorityScore` is the only place weights are applied.** Fit/Signal/
   Engagement sub-scores never change with the sliders — only the composite does.
   Enforced by a sweep invariant.
4. **Every measurement is computed once, in `lib/scoring/` and `lib/research/`.**
   The home screen's sliders reuse the exact same `priorityScore` function
   client-side; the detail page's explanation reuses the exact same
   `explainRank` function used to build the precomputed result — neither
   reimplements its logic for display.

## Data model

```ts
type Industry = 'SaaS' | 'Fintech' | 'Healthcare' | 'Retail' | 'Manufacturing' | 'Other';
type CompanySizeBucket = '1-50' | '51-200' | '201-1000' | '1000+';
type Region = 'NA' | 'EMEA' | 'APAC' | 'LATAM';
type SignalType = 'funding' | 'hiring-surge' | 'leadership-change' | 'tech-change' | 'website-change';

type Account = {
  id: string;
  name: string;
  industry: Industry;
  sizeBucket: CompanySizeBucket;
  region: Region;
  techStackMatch: boolean;
  emailOpens: number;          // trailing 90 days
  meetingsBooked: number;      // trailing 90 days
  lastTouchDaysAgo: number;    // 0 = touched today, capped at 180
};

type Signal = {
  id: string;
  accountId: string;
  type: SignalType;
  strength: 1 | 2 | 3;         // magnitude, independent of recency
  daysAgo: number;              // 0–180, relative to corpus's asOfDate
  description: string;          // templated, deterministic
};

type ScoreBreakdown = {
  fit: number;                  // 0–100
  signal: number;                // 0–100
  engagement: number;            // 0–100
};

type Weights = { fit: number; signal: number; engagement: number }; // any non-negative triple, not all zero

type ResearchBullet = { text: string; sourceSignalId: string | null }; // null = derived from firmographics/engagement, not a signal

type RankExplanation = { dominantDriver: 'fit' | 'signal' | 'engagement'; text: string };

type AccountResult = {
  account: Account;
  signals: Signal[];             // this account's own signals, sorted daysAgo ascending
  scores: ScoreBreakdown;
  priorityScore: number;         // 0–100, default weights (0.4/0.35/0.25)
  rank: number;                  // 1 = highest priorityScore at default weights, ties broken by id
  isTopPriority: boolean;        // rank <= 10
  research: ResearchBullet[];    // 3–6 bullets
  explanation: RankExplanation;
};

type FeedItem = { accountId: string; accountName: string; signal: Signal };

type CommandCenterResult = {
  generatedAt: string;
  asOfDate: string;              // fixed corpus reference date — all "daysAgo" math is relative to this, never real time
  accountCount: number;
  accounts: AccountResult[];     // sorted by rank ascending
  globalFeed: FeedItem[];        // most recent 20 signals corpus-wide, daysAgo ascending
};
```

zod-enforced at every boundary: corpus load, API response.

## Method

### The corpus's generative model

`data/generate.ts` builds each account from independently seeded draws (industry,
size bucket, region, tech-stack match, engagement stats), then attaches 0–8
signals per account, each with a `type`, an integer `strength` (1–3), a `daysAgo`
(0–180), and a templated `description` built from the account name + signal type +
a randomized magnitude detail (e.g. `"Raised $42M Series B"`, `"Posted 14 new
engineering roles in the last 30 days"`, `"Hired a new VP of Sales, previously at
a public SaaS company"`). Every draw uses a labeled, derived RNG stream (same
`Rng`/`derive` pattern as Day 019) so regenerating one part of the corpus never
perturbs another part's output relative to a fixed account id.

### The three sub-scores

```
FitScore (0–100):
  industryFit  = { SaaS: 1.0, Fintech: 0.9, Healthcare: 0.5, Retail: 0.3, Manufacturing: 0.3, Other: 0.2 }[industry]
  sizeFit      = { '1-50': 0.3, '51-200': 1.0, '201-1000': 0.8, '1000+': 0.4 }[sizeBucket]
  regionFit    = { NA: 1.0, EMEA: 0.8, APAC: 0.6, LATAM: 0.5 }[region]
  techFit      = techStackMatch ? 1.0 : 0.0

  raw = 0.35*industryFit + 0.25*sizeFit + 0.15*regionFit + 0.25*techFit
  FitScore = round(raw * 100)                     // raw ∈ [0, 1] by construction

SignalScore (0–100):
  decay(daysAgo) = max(0, 1 - daysAgo / 180)       // linear recency decay, 0 at 180 days
  rawSum = sum over this account's signals of ( strength * decay(daysAgo) )
  SignalScore = round( min(rawSum / 6, 1) * 100 )  // 6 = a 3-strength signal today plus
                                                     // a 3-strength signal at ~60% recency;
                                                     // caps rather than corpus-min-maxes so a
                                                     // single account's score never depends on
                                                     // what other accounts happen to contain

EngagementScore (0–100):
  emailEng    = min(emailOpens / 30, 1)
  meetingEng  = min(meetingsBooked / 4, 1)
  freshness   = max(0, 1 - lastTouchDaysAgo / 90)

  raw = 0.4*emailEng + 0.4*meetingEng + 0.2*freshness
  EngagementScore = round(raw * 100)

PriorityScore(breakdown, weights) (0–100):
  normalize weights so they sum to 1 (equal-thirds if all three are 0)
  PriorityScore = round( wFit*fit + wSignal*signal + wEngagement*engagement )
  default weights: { fit: 0.4, signal: 0.35, engagement: 0.25 }
```

All four formulas are pure functions over their inputs — no field references
`converted`-style ground truth, because there is no ground truth to leak here;
the point of this repo is composition and explainability, not evaluation
methodology.

### Research bullets

Deterministic, templated, and traceable — every bullet is either built directly
from one of the account's own `Signal` records (`sourceSignalId` set) or from its
firmographic/engagement fields (`sourceSignalId: null`). No bullet ever references
data absent from that account. Order: firmographic-fit bullet first, then one
bullet per signal (most recent first, capped at 4), then an engagement bullet —
clipped to at most 6, never fewer than 3 (a "no significant recent activity"
bullet fills in for accounts with zero signals).

### Rank explanation

`explainRank(breakdown, weights)` finds the sub-score contributing the largest
share of the (weighted) priority score and returns a one-sentence, templated
explanation naming that driver and its supporting facts, e.g.: *"Ranked #4 of
200 — driven primarily by recent signal activity (funding + hiring surge in the
last 30 days), with strong fit (enterprise SaaS, tech stack match) and moderate
engagement."* Ties in dominant share are broken fit → signal → engagement (a
fixed, documented order, not a random choice).

## Validation / test plan

**Unit tests** (`vitest`, `lib/**/*.test.ts`, `data/**/*.test.ts`): each scoring
formula against hand-built accounts with known expected sub-scores; weight
normalization (including the all-zero-weights edge case) against known expected
priority scores; research-bullet traceability (every bullet's `sourceSignalId` is
either `null` or an id present in that account's own signals); `explainRank`'s
tie-break order; corpus structural assertions (account count, signal count range,
id uniqueness).

**`npm run sweep`** — no network, asserting **nine invariants**:

1. **Corpus size.** Exactly 200 accounts, unique ids.
2. **Signal volume.** Every account has between 0 and 8 signals; corpus-wide
   signal count falls in a documented range; every signal's `daysAgo` is in
   `[0, 180]`.
3. **Sub-score bounds.** Every account's Fit/Signal/Engagement score is an
   integer in `[0, 100]`.
4. **Priority bounds under any weights.** For a swept set of weight triples
   (including all-on-one-factor and all-zero), every account's `priorityScore` is
   in `[0, 100]`.
5. **Weights don't touch sub-scores.** Recomputing `priorityScore` under three
   different weight triples never changes any account's Fit/Signal/Engagement
   sub-score — only the composite.
6. **Fit realism.** Accounts with full-match firmographics (top-fit industry,
   sweet-spot size, top-fit region, tech-stack match) have a strictly higher
   average FitScore than accounts with none of those four matches.
7. **Default-weight rank consistency.** The `rank` field on every `AccountResult`
   matches its position when the corpus is sorted by default-weight
   `priorityScore` descending (ties broken by id); exactly the top 10 have
   `isTopPriority: true`.
8. **Global feed correctness.** `globalFeed` is exactly the 20 most-recent signals
   corpus-wide by `daysAgo` ascending, matching a from-scratch scan of every
   account's signals.
9. **Determinism.** Running corpus generation and the full command-center pipeline
   twice over the same seed produces byte-identical output (`JSON.stringify`
   equality).

**Manual verification** before shipping: the main journey end to end on the
deployed URL — table loads sorted by priority with the top 10 badged, dragging
each weight slider visibly changes the sort order, the global feed renders and
links into account detail, an account detail page renders its breakdown chart,
timeline, research, and explanation. Failure states: an account with zero signals
still renders a valid detail page (empty timeline state, a "no recent activity"
research bullet, no crash); all three sliders dragged to 0 still produces a valid
equal-weight ranking, never `NaN` or a crash; an unknown `/accounts/[id]` renders
a clear not-found state, not a crash.

## Settled decisions

Every one of these was put to the user and confirmed before code was written.

1. **Standalone repo**, fresh synthetic dataset, zero cross-repo dependency —
   thematically linked to `icp-score`, `signal-scout`, `account-brief`,
   `tam-calculator`, `territory-builder` in the README only.
2. **Time limit is one day**, matching the rest of the series; MVP scope is a
   single, coherent two-screen dashboard, not five sub-products glued together.
3. Stack, deployment, license, and repo visibility are **identical to the sibling
   repos**: Next.js App Router, TypeScript strict, Tailwind CSS 4, zod,
   vitest/vite-node, zero dependency exceptions, deployed to Vercel, pushed to
   `github.com/akshatiwarix`, MIT license, public repo.
4. Interface is **mostly read/browse**, with one light interactive lever: three
   weight sliders that live-reweight the Priority Score and re-sort the table.
5. Corpus scale is **200 synthetic accounts**.
6. Score model is a **three-factor composite**: Fit (static firmographic ICP
   match), Signal (recency-decayed weighted sum of recent signals), Engagement
   (synthetic outreach-recency proxy) — combined into a **Priority Score** via
   user-adjustable weights, default `0.4 / 0.35 / 0.25`.
7. **Two screens**: Command Center home (sortable/filterable table + sliders +
   global signal feed) and account detail (timeline, breakdown chart, research,
   rank explanation).
8. **Research bullets are derived from the same seeded signal/firmographic
   generator** as everything else — no separate, disconnected text generator, no
   LLM call.
9. **Global cross-account signal feed** included on the home screen (most recent
   20 signals), in addition to each account's own timeline.
10. **Prioritization is the table's default sort** (Priority Score descending)
    plus a **top-10 "Top Priority" badge** — no separate queue screen.
11. Push to `main` after **every** completed task.
12. Repo is **public** on GitHub under `akshatiwarix`, matching the rest of the
    series.
